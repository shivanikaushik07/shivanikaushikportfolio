## Project overview

Budan makes coffee machines and brewing systems for the Indian market, selling both to homes and to businesses. The rebrand repositions it for a younger, design-literate buyer without giving up its premium standing.

## The challenge

Budan read as equipment. The buyer it wanted reads coffee as culture. Modernise the brand without sliding into mass-market.

## Objectives

- Reach Gen Z and younger millennials without alienating the existing base
- Move from functional appliance to design-led experience
- Build one system that holds up in both D2C and B2B

## Audience

Urban professionals, founders and creatives, 25–50, in Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi and Hyderabad. They already buy specialty beans and hop cafés. They want that cup at home, they'll pay for design, and they expect the brand to say something about them.

## Category & gap

Premium home and prosumer brewing, where appliance meets lifestyle. Metro café culture is pulling people toward machines they don't want to be trained on.

**Competitors sit at two extremes — too artisanal, or too corporate.** The middle is empty: premium but friendly, aspirational but reachable. That is the position.

```table
highlight: 1
Brand | Positioning | Brand identity & style | Strengths | Gaps vs Budan
Budan | Made-for-India coffee gear | Warm, modern, approachable, coffee-first | Indian heritage (Baba Budan), affordable quality | Needs richer visual identity and emotional storytelling
Agaro / Instacuppa | Budget appliances for home brewing | Generic, function-first | Widely available, low price | Lacks narrative, coffee lifestyle focus
De'Longhi | Global Italian espresso machines | Heritage, premium, high design | Strong legacy, trusted international brand | High price; no India-specific positioning
Kent / Wonderchef | Home appliance lifestyle brands | Clean, functional, broad kitchen aesthetic | Stylish, kitchen-friendly designs | Not coffee-centric; lacks storytelling or emotional warmth
Smeg | Premium Italian lifestyle appliance brand | Retro-modern, design-first, aspirational | Iconic aesthetics, aspirational lifestyle appeal | Not coffee-centric; machines are lifestyle statement pieces
Brunel Coffee | UK-based coffee & machine supplier (est. 1989) | Practical, service-oriented, British | Full-service offering: machines, beans, installation, training | Lacks strong branding identity; more equipment provider than lifestyle
La Marzocco | High-end, professional espresso machines (founded 1927) | Iconic, artisanal, engineering-led | Craftsmanship, precision engineering, global prestige, sustainability | Over-premium; limited approachability/emotional warmth for mass market
```

## Positioning

**A modern premium coffee machine brand for urban Indians who value craft, design, and control.**

Café-quality coffee at home and at work, through brewing systems that are thoughtfully designed and genuinely easy to use.

## Archetype

- **The Creator** — celebrates craft and precision, enables a personal ritual
- **The Explorer** — curious, expressive, design-led

## Personality & voice

Premium but never intimidating. Warm, current, confident without volume. It writes like a barista, not a brochure — sensory, plainspoken, never technical for its own sake.

## The symbol

The monogram folds two forms together — the cup-shaped **u** of the wordmark and a coffee bean — into a single stroke that reads as rising steam. Rotated four times around a centre, the same stroke becomes the brand pattern.

```figure
/works/budan/symbol-construction.jpg
u + bean → the mark, and the mark → the pattern.
```

## The wordmark

The **u** is the shape of a coffee cup — handle, saucer and all. Once it's seen it can't be unseen, which is the whole point of a mark.

```figure
/works/budan/wordmark-construction.jpg
The b and d pour; the u holds; the knob shape from the machines is built into the letterforms.
```

## Illustration & icons

One drawing language, three scales. It starts with the brewing devices themselves — espresso machine, french press, moka pot — drawn at a single line weight and issued in two colourways, blue for brand surfaces and brown for product and packaging.

```figure
/works/budan/icons.jpg
Each icon ships in both colourways; the accent is the only thing that changes.
```

The same line builds panels that tile together: the farm, the bean, the brew, the chair you drink it in. The artwork scales from a 340g bag to a full wall.
