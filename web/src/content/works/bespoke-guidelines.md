## Brand story

For more than twenty years, Dr Katherine Armour has sat across from women and listened to what was happening to their skin. Skin changes — hormones, sleep, stress, sun, time — but the skincare on offer hadn't changed with it. It was either one product for everyone, or a routine so complicated nobody kept it up.

**Skincare made for where your skin is now.**

## Founder

Katherine kept noticing the same thing: women in their 30s, 40s, 50s and beyond were changing, but skincare was still talking to them as though the only goal was to look younger. Then her own skin began to change. She understood the science, and she understood how hard it was to find products that suited what her skin actually needed. That gap became Bespoke.

## Promise, vision, values

- **Promise** — skincare made for where your skin is now
- **Vision** — a world where every stage of a woman's life is met with care, not correction

Skin changes; that's life, and we work with what it needs now. Expertise should simplify things — more products aren't better products. Women deserve facts, not fear. Healthy skin over impossible perfection. And if you don't need it, we won't tell you that you do.

## The Bespoke woman

She's probably in her 40s, 50s or 60s. Her skin has changed, and so has quite a lot else. She knows herself better than she used to, she's more discerning about what she buys, and she's less interested in doing things because she's been told she should. She isn't trying to become the woman she was ten years ago.

**She's interested in looking after the skin she's in now.**

## Personality

Bespoke knows skin. Really knows it. But expertise doesn't have to feel clinical or intimidating — this is the dermatologist you'd actually want to sit next to at dinner. We take skin seriously. We don't take ourselves quite so seriously.

## How we sound

```rows

Assured | Twenty years of dermatology means we don't overcompensate.
Warm | We talk to women, not at them.
```

## Katherine's voice

Katherine is the benchmark. She knows a lot but doesn't need you to know that she knows a lot. She talks to women, not demographics. If something works she'll explain why; if it's unnecessary she'll tell you that too. She's serious about skin, not solemn about it.

```quote
She sounds like a dermatologist in conversation, not a dermatologist making content.
```

```note
Influencer: "3 anti-ageing ingredients every woman over 40 needs."
Katherine: "There isn't a shopping list every woman suddenly needs on her 40th birthday."
```

## How we write

- Start with her, not the product — what's actually happening to her skin?
- Explain why. Understanding builds more trust than another claim.
- Use normal words. If there's a simpler way to explain the science, use it.
- Leave room to breathe. One good observation beats five claims.
- Never make ageing the enemy. Changing skin isn't something women need rescuing from.

```note
We use: changing skin · barrier support · luminosity · what skin needs now.
We avoid: fight ageing · reverse ageing · fix · correct · flawless · one size fits all.
```

Five warm accents drawn from skin and formula. They bring emphasis to a quiet green system rather than replacing it — one leads, a second may appear as type, and together they never exceed about 12% of a layout, because the skin in the frame should still be the warmest thing in it.

## Typography

Two voices. Rules carries the wordmark, headlines, body, labels and everything functional — regular and medium only, never bold. Libre Baskerville is the highlight: italic by default, used for a single emphasised phrase, a pull quote or a campaign line. If a layout has more than one Baskerville element, remove one.

## Photography

We photograph skin the way it actually is. Texture stays, lines stay, tone varies. Light is cool and even, so skin reads warm against it and nothing has to be corrected in the grade. The reason is simple: Bespoke supports skin through change rather than fixing it, and a retouched face argues the opposite of everything we say.

```rows
Skin macro | Cheek, eye, temple, lip. No makeup, no retouching.
The considered portrait | Women 50s to 70s, plain ground, grey hair as it is, faces at rest.
Woman and product | She holds it, at human scale, never floating on a plinth.
Formula | A smear, a swatch, a butter melting. One soft light, no props.
Type on image | Set straight onto skin or product. One line, one idea.
```
