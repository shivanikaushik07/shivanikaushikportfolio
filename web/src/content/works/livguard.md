## Project overview

Livguard sells energy storage and power solutions across India. A full responsive redesign of the website — brand, education and decision-assist, with no checkout at the end of it. Senior Manager, UI/UX, leading an eight-person team across brand, design and engineering. Twenty-one days.

## The challenge

Long, dense pages were losing people before the product could. A first-time buyer in a Tier 3 city, on a phone, in their own language, had to self-serve through a wide and specification-heavy range — and there was no cart at the end to measure success against.

## What we were solving

```rows
Scroll depth | Users dropped off within the first few sections.
Cognitive overload | Specification arrived before reassurance.
Product discovery | A wide range with no guidance through it.
No checkout | The UX had to build confidence, not conversion.
```

## Who it's for

Homeowners, first-time buyers and small business owners, 25–55, urban and semi-urban, mostly on mobile. Mixed comfort with English and a wide spread of technical literacy. They want reassurance before specifications and a guided path over free browsing.

Commercial buyers and channel partners are the second audience, and they want the opposite — direct access to the spec sheet without being walked there.

## What the data said

- Roughly 65–70% of traffic on mobile; desktop skewed to comparison and spec review
- Drop-off concentrated past the first few scroll sections
- Time spent on product pages without progressing further
- Traffic spread across Tier 1, 2 and 3 — a clear case for regional language
- Category benchmark: guided selection beats free browsing, and the purchase still closes offline

```note
Behavioural figures are directional, drawn from platform analytics and category benchmarks.
```

## Strategy

```rows
Progressive disclosure | Long pages broken into modular sections; technical depth on demand through accordions, tabs and cards.
Use-case led discovery | Products reorganised by application, home and commercial, instead of by specification.
Recommendation engine | Plain-language questions, logic-driven output, framed as assistance rather than a sales funnel.
Multi-language by design | Built for text expansion, icons over idiom, language switch always within reach.
```

## Design system

Clean and trust-forward: brand colour balanced against neutral layouts, strong hierarchy, real whitespace. Card-based product modules, one CTA hierarchy, sections built to scale. Mobile-first content order, thumb-reachable targets, reduced text density on small screens.

## Heuristics check

The build was audited against Nielsen's heuristics before handoff. Information architecture, product discovery and error prevention came out strong; multi-language UX and accessibility were the two areas left with room, largely down to the timeline.

## Outcome

No quantitative tracking was in place, which is the honest limitation of this project. What shipped was a cleaner, more modern brand surface, clearer product discovery, lower cognitive load for first-time users, and a UX framework the team has kept building on since.
