## Project overview

A hiring dashboard embedded inside growthjockey.com, replacing a six-tool patchwork with one surface. Product Designer, six weeks from discovery to ship, with two engineers and an HR lead.

## The brief was blunt

HR was running hiring across Google Sheets, email threads, LinkedIn DMs and third-party job boards — five or six tools, each with its own login, its own copy of the truth, and its own reason to go stale.

```quote
Bring the entire hiring process onto our own website. Keep it under seven steps.
— The whole product spec, and the sharpest constraint of the project
```

## What discovery surfaced

Four stakeholders, three shadowed hiring cycles.

```rows
Posting a job | Re-typing the same details into three or four boards.
Applicant status | Lived in a spreadsheet nobody trusted after Wednesday.
Interview scheduling | Happened in personal inboxes, invisible to the team.
Weekly standup | "Where does this role stand?" had no shared answer.
Manager updates | HR compiled reports HR didn't have time to compile.
```

## A calm, dark command center

Four KPI cards, the hiring-stats chart with its live applicants / hired / rejected ratio, a spotlight candidate, and a recent-jobs table. No scrolling required to answer *is hiring on track?*

## Three steps. That's the whole flow.

Basic Information → Candidate Requirements → Preview. Each stage fits on one screen, radio cards give immediate feedback, and the description editor appears only when it's needed.

## A review page, not a submit page

Every section of the final step — job detail, location, compensation, requirements, description, skills — carries an inline edit. HR never walks back through the wizard to correct a typo.

## Open positions left, applicants right

Every open role is a row answering the four questions HR gets asked most: how many applied, when it was posted, when it closes — the deadline doubles as a progress bar — and the shortlist. Moving a candidate from Shortlisted to Contacted to Interview to Hired happens inline through the stage dropdown. No modal, no navigation, no lost context.

## Everything about a candidate, on one panel

The applicant detail panel slides over the list rather than away from it, so HR never loses their place in the queue. Basic Info, Resume and Hiring Pipeline are quiet tabs; Send Message and Send Email sit top-right in the brand blue.

## Three months later

A follow-up review with the HR team, plus workflow analytics from the platform.

```metrics
cols: 4
−68% | Time to post a new role
−54% | Time to move a candidate a stage
100% | HR tasks on one surface
4.6/5 | HR satisfaction, internal survey
```

```note
Steps to post a role: 3, down from 14. Steps to hire end to end: 7, down from 31.
```

```quote
Managers stopped pinging me for updates — the dashboard already answered them.
— HR Lead, three-month review
```

The qualitative shift mattered more than the numbers: HR's role moved from coordinator of information to evaluator of candidates. The seven-step ceiling was the single most useful constraint of the project — it forced every "what if we added…" to be traded against the steps it would cost.
